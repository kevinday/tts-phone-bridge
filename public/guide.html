TTS Phone Bridge — Guide

This app lets you take part in phone calls and video meetings using a synthesized clone of your own voice. You type; the other person hears you speak.

Daily use

  • Type and press Enter — your text is spoken right away.
  • Auto-send — with "Auto-send on .?!" checked, finishing a sentence with a period, question mark, or exclamation point (plus a space) sends it automatically, so you never touch Enter mid-conversation.
  • Quick phrases — one-click buttons for common replies ("Yes", "Could you repeat that?"). Click ✎ Edit to customize.
  • Recent messages — the strip above the typing area shows the last few things you said. 🔊 means it played; ⚠️ means it failed and the other person did not hear it. Click any line to say it again.
  • Repeat — re-plays your last message. Stop (or Esc) cuts off speech immediately.
  • Speed — slider in the top bar, 0.7×–1.2×. Double-click to reset. Most people like 0.85–0.95×; the extremes can degrade audio quality.

Choosing where the voice plays (Output)

Click the Output name in the top bar. If you've saved your devices, you'll see two buttons — 📞 Phone call and 💻 Meeting — and switching takes one click. "More devices…" shows the full list, where the 📞/💻 buttons next to each device let you save it as a quick button.

Setup for phone calls (hardware)

Phone calls need a wired path from the computer into the phone. The recommended chain (verified working with a Google Pixel): 

Computer headphone jack (3.5mm)
   ↓ 3.5mm cable
Attenuator cable (-30dB)        ← drops line level down to mic level
   ↓
USB-C audio interface, MIC IN   (Saramonic SR-EA2U, ~$45)
   ↓ its USB-C plug
Android phone

Your earbuds plug into the interface's HEADPHONE OUT jack — that's where you hear the caller.

PartPurpose
Saramonic SR-EA2U USB-C audio interface The phone sees it as a USB headset, so call audio routes through it — both directions.
Line-to-mic attenuator cable (e.g. Cubilux, switchable -10/-20/-30dB) The computer's headphone output is ~30× too loud for a mic input; this drops it down. Start at -30dB.
3.5mm aux cableComputer → attenuator.
Wired earbudsPlug into the SR-EA2U to hear the caller.
Phone compatibility: Google Pixel phones route cellular-call audio through USB audio devices reliably. Samsung phones generally do not — they restrict call audio to approved headsets. Test with a voice-memo recording before relying on it for a real call.

First-time test

  1. Connect everything, select the output in the app (📞 Phone call).
  2. Record a voice memo on the phone while playing TTS from the app.
  3. Distorted? Use more attenuation (-30dB) or lower computer volume. Too quiet? Less attenuation (-20dB) or raise computer volume.
  4. Then do a live test call with a friend.

Setup for Teams / Google Meet / Zoom

Meetings don't use the phone hardware at all — the voice is routed in software through a virtual audio cable, which the meeting app reads as your microphone.

Windows (one-time install)

  1. Install VB-Cable (free), as Administrator, and reboot.
  2. In this app: Output → CABLE Input (VB-Audio Virtual Cable) — save it as your 💻 Meeting device.
  3. In Teams/Meet/Zoom: set the microphone to CABLE Output (VB-Audio Virtual Cable).

Hearing your own voice in meetings (optional)

Install VoiceMeeter (free) and set its outputs: A1 → CABLE Input, A2 → your headphones, with both A1+A2 lit on the VoiceMeeter Input strip. Then set this app's output to VoiceMeeter Input instead. Your voice goes to the meeting and your headphones at the same time.

Mac

Use BlackHole (free, 2ch) as the virtual cable: app output → BlackHole 2ch, meeting microphone → BlackHole 2ch.

Troubleshooting

ProblemTry this
Caller hears nothing (phone) Check the app's Output is the phone chain (📞). Check every cable is fully seated, especially attenuator → MIC IN. Confirm the phone shows a headset/USB-audio icon during the call.
Meeting hears nothing App Output must be the virtual cable (💻), and the meeting's microphone must be the matching cable device (e.g. CABLE Output). Both must be set — they're two halves of one wire.
Voice is distorted/crackly Lower the computer volume or increase attenuation (-30dB).
Voice too quiet for the caller Raise computer volume or reduce attenuation (-20dB).
You can't hear the caller Earbuds go in the SR-EA2U's HEADPHONE OUT, not the computer. Check its headphone volume dial.
A message shows ⚠️ in Recent messages It never played — the other person didn't hear it. Click it to retry. If it keeps failing, check your internet connection and ElevenLabs key.
Hum/buzz on calls Likely a ground loop. A 3.5mm ground-loop isolator (~$10) in the chain fixes it.
Device names are blank in the picker The browser hides names until microphone permission is granted once — allow it when prompted (the app never records).

Good to know

  • There's a ~0.3–0.5s delay between sending and the voice starting — the time the voice synthesis takes. The TTFB number in the top bar shows it.
  • Your ElevenLabs API key is stored only in this browser. Don't use the app on a shared computer.
  • The app works offline as an installed PWA, but speaking requires an internet connection (the voice is synthesized in the cloud).
